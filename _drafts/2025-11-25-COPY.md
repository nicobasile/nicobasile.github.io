Over the past year, I’ve been developing an individual research project aimed at answering a question that has been sitting at the core of modern embodied AI:

**Can a single Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model, trained on a blend of real teleoperation data and high‑quality synthetic trajectories, learn temporally consistent, instruction‑conditioned robotic control comparable to emerging frontier systems like Gemini Robotics and GR‑3?**

This blog post summarizes the full technical arc of the project — from teleoperation data collection, to synthetic trajectory generation, to VLA fine‑tuning, and finally to GRPO reinforcement learning in MuJoCo/MJX.

## 🎯 Project Overview

Between January 2024 and today, I built, trained, and evaluated a robotics foundation model with the following goals:

* **Improve embodied grounding** using real + synthetic teleoperation data.
* **Strengthen temporal consistency** over long-horizon tasks.
* **Align policy behavior** to natural-language instructions.
* **Push toward state-aligned behavior** similar to Gemini Robotics / GR‑3 style pipelines.

**Core Result:**
A fine-tuned Vision-Language-Action model that exhibits *smooth, instruction-conditioned behavior* and maintains trajectory-level temporal stability, with reinforcement-learned refinements that improve state-aligned action execution.

## 🧩 Architecture: Vision-Language-Action (VLA)

The base model follows the modern VLA design pattern:

* **Vision Encoder:** A ViT-derived encoder producing spatial features.
* **Language Encoder:** A lightweight LLM or instruction encoder for task prompts.
* **Action Decoder / Policy Head:** Predicts either direct joint velocities or normalized action vectors.

The central idea is to unify these three modalities so that the robot can:

1. **Perceive** (vision),
2. **Interpret** (language), and
3. **Execute** (actions)

…in a single forward pass.

## 📦 Data: Real + Synthetic Teleoperation Trajectories

One of the biggest challenges with robotics learning is obtaining enough high‑quality trajectory data.
I approached this using a hybrid strategy:

### **1. Real Teleoperation Data**

I collected (and cleaned) a set of demonstrations using a 6‑DoF arm:

* Pick-and-place tasks
* Drawer opening
* Object pushing
* Button pressing
* Multi-step sequences

This dataset forms the *ground-truth anchor* for embodiment, grounding, and motion smoothness.

### **2. Synthetic Trajectory Expansion**

To scale beyond limited teleop hours, I generated synthetic trajectories using:

* A diffusion-based motion model for action rollout
* Physics-consistent interpolation in MuJoCo
* A perturbation model to introduce mild stochasticity and robustness

Synthetic data offered:

* More variety
* Longer horizons
* Harder perturbations
* Rare edge cases (failures, recoveries)

## 🧪 Fine‑Tuning the VLA Model

The fine‑tuning process used supervised behavioral cloning over both datasets:

* Loss:

  * **Action MSE / cosine similarity** (depending on head type)
  * **Temporal smoothness loss**
  * **Vision-Language alignment loss** (contrastive)

* Batch sampling was **stratified** across tasks to avoid mode collapse toward simpler trajectories.

This stage improved:

* Embodied grounding
* Coherence between language and action
* Smoothness over long horizons

## 🧗 Reinforcement Learning: GRPO in MuJoCo/MJX

After supervised fine‑tuning, I added a reinforcement optimization phase using:

**Generalized Reinforcement Policy Optimization (GRPO)**
— a variant of PPO adapted for hybrid state‑action spaces.

The RL environment setup included:

* **MJX** (the JAX-accelerated MuJoCo backend)
* Reward terms for:

  * Goal proximity
  * Collision avoidance
  * Action smoothness
  * Instruction satisfaction

Key advantage of GRPO:
It corrects for compounding errors in BC policies and enforces *state-aligned* behavior.

In practice, I observed:

* More stable trajectories
* Higher task success rates
* Faster recovery from perturbations

## 📌 Key Learnings

* Synthetic data is extremely effective when grounded by real demonstrations.
* VLA models naturally benefit from multimodal supervision, especially language alignment.
* GRPO reinforcement learning is critical for eliminating drift and compounding BC errors.
* Even small-scale experiments can meaningfully approximate frontier robotics behaviors.
